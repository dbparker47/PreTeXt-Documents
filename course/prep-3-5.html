    Objectives

    For the content of this class, you will be expected to be able to do the following:
    🔗
    • Explain the definition of a Hamiltonian cycle.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Determine whether or not a given graph has an Eulerian trail or Eulerian circuit based on the degrees of the vertices.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Construct a Hamiltonian cycle on a given graph, if it is possible.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Prove theorems about Eulerian trails & circuits, and about Hamiltonian paths & cycles.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    In-Class Activities 32. Eulerian Circuit Algorithm.

    Suppose we have a connected graph such that every vertex has even degree. We will create an algorithm to construct an Eulerian circuit.
    🔗
    1. Choose an arbitrary vertex \(v_0\text{.}\) What if no edges are incident to \(v_0\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If we are not finished, choose any edge incident to \(v_0\) that we have not yet traveled on and travel along it to another vertex \(v_1\) along this edge. Why can we do this?
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Keep repeating step 2 until we cannot do it anymore.
      1. Why will we eventually have to stop?
        🔗
        🔗
      2. Prove that when we stop, we are on the vertex \(v_0\text{.}\) Hint: Assume we are on any other vertex and find a contradiction.
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    4. So we have the circuit \(v_0v_1 \cdots v_nv_0\text{.}\) What do we do if we have traveled along each edge in the graph?
      🔗
      🔗
    5. What if we have some edges not yet traveled along, but none of them are incident to any of \(v_0\text{,}\) \(v_1, \ldots , v_n\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Suppose one of the vertices on our circuit is incident to an unused edge. Relabel our vertices so that this one is \(v_0\) and our circuit is \(v_0v_1 \cdots v_nv_0\text{.}\) Why is it safe to assume that \(v_0\) is the first vertex in the circuit?
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Repeat steps 2-4 to create a new circuit \(v_0w_1w_2 \cdots w_mv_0\text{.}\) Again, think about why we can do this.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. Take the circuits we created in step 3 and step 7 and turn them into a single circuit.
      🔗
      🔗
    9. Repeat steps 4-8 until we are forced to stop.
      🔗
      🔗
    10. Can this process go on forever? If not, what happens when the process stops?
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    In-Class Activities 33. Hamiltonian Cycles.

    Work the following problems related to Hamiltonian cycles.
    🔗
    1. Determine the values of \(m\) and \(n\) for which of the following have Hamiltonian cycles.
      1. \(\displaystyle K_n\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(\displaystyle K_{m,n}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Suppose that \(G\) has a Hamiltonian cycle. Prove that \(G\) is connected.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Let \(G\) be the graph pictured below. Prove that \(G\) does not have a Hamiltonian cycle.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. (Not on quiz) Let \(G_1=(V_1,E_1)\) and \(G_2=(V_2,E_2)\) be graphs. Let \(v \in V_1\) and \(w \in V_2\text{.}\) Define the graph \(H\) whose vertex set is \(V_1 \cup V_2 \cup \{ u \}\) and whose edge set is \(E_1 \cup E_2 \cup \{ uv, uw \}\text{.}\) Prove that \(H\) does not have a Hamiltonian cycle.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. (Not on quiz) Let \(G_1=(V_1,E_1)\) and \(G_2=(V_2,E_2)\) be graphs with Hamiltonian cycles. Let \(v_1,v_2 \in V_1\text{,}\) and let \(w_1,w_2 \in V_2\) such that \(v_1v2\) is one of the edges of a Hamiltonian cycle for \(G_1\text{,}\) and \(w_1w_2\) is one of the edges of a Hamiltonian cycle for \(G_2\text{.}\) Define the graph $H$ whose vertex set is \(V_1 \cup V_2\) and whose edge set is \(E_1 \cup E_2 \cup \{ v_1w_1, v_2w_2 \}\text{.}\) Prove that \(H\) has a Hamiltonian cycle.
      🔗
      🔗
