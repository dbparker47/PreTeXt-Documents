    Worksheet Exam 1 - Math 325
    

    For each of the following problems, you will state the solution of the problem as a theorem, and you will write a proof of the theorem. Keep the following in mind.
    • All proofs must be written in complete sentences.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If computations are a part of your proof, they must be written as part of the proof. Do not separate the computations from the explanations.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your proof may not have any unproved steps. All steps must be justified by definitions, your previously proven steps, or by facts we proved in class or in videos.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    1.

    (25 pts.) Suppose \(a \equiv 7\) (mod 9) and \(b \equiv 5\) (mod 9). Prove that \(ab \equiv 8\) (mod 9).
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    (25 pts.) Let \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) and \(c\) be integers. Prove that if \(a \mid b\text{,}\) and \(a\) does not divide \(c\text{,}\) then \(a\) does not divide \(b+c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    (25 pts.) Suppose that \(x\) is a nonzero rational number and \(y\) is an irrational number (i.e. a real number that is not a rational number). Prove that \(xy\) is an irrational number.
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    (25 pts.) Suppose you have a system of buildings where each pair of buildings is connected by a one-way road. Prove that there is a route that enables you to visit every building.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Worksheet Exam 2 - Math 325
    

    For each of the following problems, you will state the solution of the problem as a theorem, and you will write a proof of the theorem. Keep the following in mind.
    • All proofs must be written in complete sentences.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If computations are a part of your proof, they must be written as part of the proof. Do not separate the computations from the explanations.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your proof may not have any unproved steps. All steps must be justified by definitions, your previously proven steps, or by facts we proved in class or in videos.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    1.

    (25 pts.) Suppose you have a set \(A\text{,}\) and let \(A_1\text{,}\) \(A_2, \ldots , A_n\) be a collection of subsets of \(A\) with the following properties.
    • \(\displaystyle A = A_1 \cup A_2 \cup \cdots \cup A_n\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • There are no elements of \(A\) that are in more than one of the \(A_i\)’s (i.e. if \(i \ne j\text{,}\) then \(A_i \cap A_j = \emptyset\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    Define the relation \(R\) as follows. For \(a,b \in A\text{,}\) we say \(aRb\) when \(a\) and \(b\) are in the same \(A_i\) (i.e. there exists \(i\) such that \(a,b \in A_i\)). Prove that \(R\) is an equivalence relation.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Define the relation \(\mid\) on \({\mathbb N} \backslash \{0,1\}\) (i.e. the natural numbers without 0 and 1) by \(a \mid b\) precisely when \(a\) divides \(b\text{.}\) Note that we are excluding 0 and 1 from the numbers we are considering for the relation.
    🔗
    (a)
    (12 pts.) Prove that \(\mid\) is a partial order.
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    (7 pts.) Prove that \(\mid\) has no maximal elements.
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    (6 pts.) Prove that every prime number is a minimal element for \(\mid\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    (25 pts.) Consider the relations \(E_9\) and \(E_{12}\) on the set of integers defined as follows.
    • \(aE_9b\) precisely when \(a \equiv b\) (mod 9).
      🔗
      🔗
    • \(aE_{12}b\) precisely when \(a \equiv b\) (mod 12).
      🔗
      🔗
    Let \(R=E_9 \circ E_{12}\text{.}\) Suppose that \(2Rx\text{.}\) Prove that \(x \equiv 2\) (mod 3).
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    (25 pts.) Let \(R\) be a relation on the set \(A\text{,}\) and let \(R^+\) be the transitive closure of \(R\text{.}\) Let \(a_1, a_2, \ldots , a_m \in A\text{,}\) and suppose that for all \(1 \le k \le m-1\) we have \(a_k R a_{k+1}\) (in directed graph form, this is \(a_1 \rightarrow a_2 \rightarrow a_3 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow a_m\)). Prove by induction that \(a_1 R^+ a_k\) for all \(2 \le k \le m\) (i.e. \(a_1 \rightarrow a_k\) for all \(2 \le k \le m\) for \(R^+)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Worksheet Exam 3 - Math 325
    

    For each of the following problems, you will state the solution of the problem as a theorem, and you will write a proof of the theorem. Keep the following in mind.
    • All proofs must be written in complete sentences.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If computations are a part of your proof, they must be written as part of the proof. Do not separate the computations from the explanations.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your proof may not have any unproved steps. All steps must be justified by definitions, your previously proven steps, or by facts we proved in class or in videos.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    1.

    (30 pts.) Let \(G\) be a connected graph that contains a cycle \(v_1v_2 \ldots v_nv_1\text{.}\) Prove that if you remove one of the edges in the cycle, the resulting graph is connected.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(T\) be a tree with at least two vertices, and consider the following algorithm applied to \(T\text{.}\)
    1. Choose a random vertex in \(T\text{.}\) Call it current_vertex.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. If possible, choose a vertex that is adjacent to current_vertex that has not been visited yet; call this new vertex adjacent_vertex. If it is not possible, stop the algorithm and return the vertex current_vertex.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. If the algorithm has not yet been stopped, change the name of adjacent_vertex to current_vertex and repeat step (2) above.
      🔗
      🔗
    Prove that the algorithm eventually terminates and that the output of the algorithm is a leaf (i.e. a vertex of degree 1). Hint: I suggest letting \(v_1, v_2, \ldots , v_n\) be the vertices that are visited (in order), where \(v_n\) is the output of the algorithm. I would suggest a proof by contradiction.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    (40 pts.) Choose one of the following. You will likely need to use the theorem you proved in #1 on this exam in your proof.
    🔗
    (a)
    Let \(T\) be a tree you get from Kruskal’s algorithm, and let \(\{ e_1, e_2, \ldots , e_r\}\) be the edges of \(T\) listed in the order you choose them in Kruskal’s algorithm. Let \(T_m,\) be a minimum spanning tree, and let \(e_k\) be the first edge of \(T\) that is not in \(T_m\) (if such an edge exists).
    🔗
    (i)
    Prove that adding \(e_k\) to \(T_m\) creates a cycle.
    🔗
    🔗
    (ii)
    Prove that there is some edge \(e\) in \(T_m\) but not in \(T\) such that if you remove \(e\) and replace it with \(e_k\text{,}\) the resulting graph is a spanning tree.
    🔗
    🔗
    (iii)
    Prove that \(w(e_k) \geq w(e)\text{.}\) Hint: \(T_m\) is a minimum spanning tree, so what does that tell you about the spanning tree from (b)?
    🔗
    🔗
    (iv)
    Prove that \(w(e_k) \leq w(e)\text{.}\) Hint: At the \(k^{\hbox{th}}\) step of Kruskal’s algorithm, would it be appropriate to choose \(e\) instead of \(e_k\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    (v)
    What happens to the weight of \(T_m\) if you replace \(e\) with \(e_k\text{?}\) Explain how you would continue this process to show \(T\) is a minimum spanning tree.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Let \(T\) be a tree you get from Prim’s algorithm, and let \(\{ e_1, e_2, \ldots , e_r\}\) be the edges of \(T\) listed in the order you choose them in Prim’s algorithm. Let \(T_m,\) be a minimum spanning tree
    🔗
    (i)
    Suppose \(e_1\) is not in \(T\text{.}\) Prove that adding \(e_1\) to \(T_m\) creates a cycle.
    🔗
    🔗
    (ii)
    Prove that \(e_1\) is in a minimum spanning tree. Hint: Remove a carefully chosen edge from the cycle you created in (a).
    🔗
    🔗
    (iii)
    Using a similar technique as in (a) and (b), prove that \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) are part of a common minimum spanning tree.
    🔗
    🔗
    (iv)
    Prove that \(e_1\text{,}\) \(e_2\text{,}\) and \(e_3\) are part of a common minimum spanning tree.
    🔗
    🔗
    (v)
    Prove that all \(e_i\) form a minimum spanning tree by induction.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Worksheet Final Exam - Math 325
    

    For each of the following problems, you will state the solution of the problem as a theorem, and you will write a proof of the theorem. Keep the following in mind.
    • All proofs must be written in complete sentences.
      🔗
      🔗
    • If computations are a part of your proof, they must be written as part of the proof. Do not separate the computations from the explanations.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Your proof may not have any unproved steps. All steps must be justified by definitions, your previously proven steps, or by facts we proved in class or in videos.
      🔗
      🔗
    For both of the following, let \(G\) be a connected weighted graph where we are trying to solve the Route Inspection Problem.
    🔗

    1.

    (30 pts.) Suppose we have two pairs of vertices, \(\{ v_1,w_1 \}\) and \(\{ v_2,w_2 \}\text{,}\) where each of \(v_1\text{,}\) \(v_2\text{,}\) \(w_1\) and \(w_2\) have odd degree. Suppose the shortest paths between \(v_1\) & \(w_1\) and between \(v_2\) & \(w_2\) go through the same edge \(uv\text{.}\) Prove that these pairings of the vertices cannot be part of a solution to the Route Inspection Problem. Hint: Prove by contradiction, and let \(d_1\) be the weight of the shortest from \(v_1\) to \(uv\text{,}\) \(d_2\) be the weight of the shortest path from \(uv\) to \(v_2\text{,}\) \(d_3\) be the weight of the shortest path from \(w_1\) to \(uv\) and \(d_4\) be the weight of the shortest path from \(uv\) to \(w_2\text{.}\) Consider the following questions.
    🔗
    (a)
    What is the total distance covered by the shortest paths between these two pairs?
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Can you find a pairing of the vertices that gives a shorter distance?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    (30 pts.) Suppose \(G\) has precisely four vertices (say \(u\text{,}\) \(v\text{,}\) \(w\text{,}\) and \(x\)) with odd degree. Let \(P_1\) be a path between \(u\) and \(v\text{,}\) and let \(P_2\) be a path between \(w\) and \(x\text{.}\) Suppose also that \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) share no edges. Prove that duplicating the edges of \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) transforms \(G\) into an Eulerian multigraph.
    🔗
    🔗
